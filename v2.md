## 1. Executive Summary Locks In the Official 8-Slide Specification

Version 1 (V1) establishes a deterministic, mathematically rigorous, and fully tested Python optimization and visualization engine that ingests an unseen [ResPlan floor plan JSON](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.kaggle.com%2Fdatasets%2Fresplan%2Fresplan) and automatically decides four coupled outputs with zero manual adjustment:

1. **Hub Count & Placement:** How many hubs ($H$) are needed and the exact metric $(x, y)$ coordinate where each hub goes (inside an assigned eligible room, $\ge 0.5\text{ m}$ from walls).
2. **Contiguous Room & Node Assignment:** Which eligible rooms (`living`, `bedroom`, `kitchen`, `storage`) and fixed interior nodes ($N_i$) each hub serves, forming a single connected zone through doors and open passages.
3. **Closed-Ring Node Sequencing:** The visiting order $H_k \to N_{k,1} \to N_{k,2} \to \dots \to N_{k,n} \to H_k$ for each hub's closed, non-branching ring.
4. **Axis-Aligned Physical Cable Routing:** The exact horizontal and vertical (rectilinear) polylines in metres that pass between rooms exclusively through doors and open passages, stay strictly inside the `inner` boundary outside `wall` and `window` polygons, and return to the starting hub.

### 2.2 The 6 Hard Validity Rules (Slide 3 — Breaking Any Rule Scores Zero)

Every generated design must pass all 6 hard rules before it is ranked:

1. **Rule 1 — Coverage:** Every eligible room and its fixed node is assigned to **exactly one** hub; none omitted, split, or duplicated.
2. **Rule 2 — Capacity:** Each hub serves at most $100\text{ m}^2$ of eligible-room area and at most $5$ nodes.
3. **Rule 3 — Zone Contiguity:** A hub's rooms are connected through **doors or open passages only**. Windows, wall-only adjacency, and the building entrance (`front_door`) do not count.
4. **Rule 4 — Closed Ring:** One closed ring per hub: $H_k \to \text{every assigned node once} \to \text{same } H_k$, with no branches and never ending at another hub. Retracing a door to reach a single-door room is allowed; shared collinear line length counts as overlap ($O$).
5. **Rule 5 — Free Space & Axis-Alignment:** Cable stays strictly inside the `inner` boundary, outside `wall` and `window` polygons, runs **only horizontally or vertically** ($\Delta x = 0$ or $\Delta y = 0$), and passes between rooms only through door or open-passage gaps.
6. **Rule 6 — Hub Point Clearance:** Every hub point lies inside an eligible room assigned to that hub, **at least $0.5\text{ m}$ from walls**. A hub and a node may share a room.

### 2.3 Official Evaluation Metrics, Checker Tolerance & Ranking Hierarchy (Slides 3, 6 & 7)

The automated evaluation checker ignores self-reported summary metrics and recomputes every metric directly from the submitted polylines using a **$1\text{ mm}$ ($0.001\text{ m}$) geometric tolerance** (points within $1\text{ mm}$ are treated as the same point; parallel lines within $1\text{ mm}$ are treated as the same line):

1. **Primary Rank — Fewest Hubs ($H$):** Valid designs are always ranked by minimum hub count $H$ first.
2. **Secondary Rank — Lowest Cost $J$ (in metres):**
   $$J = L + 5X + O + 0.5B$$
   where each metric is summed over all rings in the plan:
   - **Cable Length ($L$, weight $1.0\text{ per m}$):** Sum of polyline lengths in metres over every leg of every ring, return legs included. A retraced stretch counts each time it is traversed ($3\text{ m out} + 3\text{ m back} = 6\text{ m}$).
   - **Crossings ($X$, weight $5.0\text{ each}$):** Number of points where two cables meet **away from a node or hub** ($> 1\text{ mm}$ from any node or hub), counted inside a single ring and between different rings, once per intersection point. Legs meeting at a node or hub do **not** count as crossings ($X = 0$).
   - **Overlap ($O$, weight $1.0\text{ per m}$):** Total length in metres where cable lies on top of other cable on the **same line** (within $1\text{ mm}$ lateral distance), such as a direct retrace. A segment covered by $k$ legs counts $(k - 1)$ times its length. **Parallel runs on different lines ($> 1\text{ mm}$ apart, e.g., $20\text{ mm}$ offset through a single-door room) do NOT count as overlap ($O = 0$)!**
   - **Bends ($B$, weight $0.5\text{ each}$):** Direction changes along each closed ring, **nodes and hubs included**. Because cables are axis-aligned, every $90^\circ$ turn counts as $B = 1$, a $180^\circ$ reversal at a node/hub/vertex counts once ($B = 1$), and passing straight through a node or hub counts $0$ ($B = 0$).
3. **Tertiary Tie-Breaker — Balanced Hub Area Gap:** Equal $J$ is broken by a **smaller assigned-area gap between hubs**:
   $$\Delta A_{\text{gap}} = \max_{k \in \{1..H\}} \text{Area}(H_k) - \min_{k \in \{1..H\}} \text{Area}(H_k)$$

## 5. Six-Phase Engineering Plan: Goals, Build Specs, Test Gates, Integration & Reviews

### Phase 1: Scale Conversion, Polygon Healing, Eligible Room Indexing & Fixed Node Loading

#### 5.1.1 Phase 1 Goal
Ingest a raw organiser/ResPlan JSON file in drawing units, apply the exact Slide 4 scale conversion (`scale = sqrt(inner.area / area)`) once at the start, repair invalid polygons, classify eligible vs. excluded rooms, construct the `inner - wall - window` free-space geometry, and load the fixed interior node coordinates ($0.5\text{ m}$ from walls).

#### 5.1.2 What & How to Build in Phase 1
1. **`scale_converter.py` (Slide 4 Metric Conversion):**
   - Parse `plan["inner"]` into a [Shapely](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fshapely.readthedocs.io%2Fen%2Fstable%2Freference%2Fshapely.Polygon.html) polygon, repair if needed via `shapely.make_valid()`, and read `plan["area"]` (never `net_area`).
   - Compute `scale = math.sqrt(inner_poly.area / float(plan["area"]))`.
   - Transform every geometry in the plan from drawing units to metres via `shapely.affinity.scale(geom, xfact=1.0/scale, yfact=1.0/scale, origin=(0, 0))` and translate if needed so the bounding box origin is lower-left with $y$ pointing up.
2. **`parser.py` (Room Decomposition & Free-Space Extraction):**
   - Extract polygons for eligible categories (`living`, `bedroom`, `kitchen`, `storage`) and excluded categories (`bathroom`, `balcony`, `stair`, `garden`, `pool`, `parking`, `land`).
   - Heal self-intersections with `make_valid()` and filter out degenerate slivers ($< 2.0\text{ m}^2$ per Slide 3 benchmark note).
   - Assign canonical room IDs (`R01`, `R02`, ...) or preserve the organiser's room IDs if provided in the JSON.
   - Construct the impassable obstacle union $\mathcal{O} = \text{wall} \cup \text{window} \cup \text{front\_door}$ (plus exterior space outside `inner`) and the routable free space $\mathcal{F} = \text{inner} \setminus (\text{wall} \cup \text{window})$.
3. **`node_loader.py` (Fixed Node Ingestion & $0.5\text{ m}$ Clearance Verification):**
   - Load each room's fixed node `N1, N2, ...` directly from the input JSON (`units / scale`) and verify that it lies inside its host eligible room at $\ge 0.5\text{ m}$ clearance from walls (within $1\text{ mm}$ tolerance).
   - If running on a raw ResPlan dataset file that does not yet have pre-populated node coordinates, compute the $0.5\text{ m}$ wall-eroded room interior $P_i^{\text{safe}} = P_i \setminus \mathcal{O}.\text{buffer}(0.50)$ and place the fixed node deterministically via `shapely.ops.polylabel(P_i_safe, tolerance=0.001)`.

#### 5.1.3 Phase 1 Verification Gate (Unit Tests & Invariant Passes)
Run `pytest tests/test_phase1_ingestion.py` to verify:
- **Test 1.1 (`test_slide4_exact_scale_conversion`):** Given a plan with $200 \times 180$ drawing units, `inner.area = 36000`, and `area = 110.0`, assert `scale == pytest.approx(18.09068, rel=1e-4)`, converted width $\approx 11.055\text{ m}$, height $\approx 9.950\text{ m}$, and $0.5\text{ m}$ clearance equals $\approx 9.045$ drawing units.
- **Test 1.2 (`test_net_area_ignored`):** Set `net_area = 0` in the input JSON and assert the scale converter succeeds without error using `area`.
- **Test 1.3 (`test_eligible_and_excluded_rooms`):** Verify that every `living`, `bedroom`, `kitchen`, and `storage` room has exactly 1 fixed node, and `bathroom`, `balcony`, `stair`, `garden`, `pool`, `parking`, and `land` have 0 nodes.
- **Test 1.4 (`test_fixed_node_immutability_and_clearance`):** Verify that input node coordinates are never modified by downstream functions and lie inside `inner` at distance $\ge 0.5\text{ m} - 0.001\text{ m}$ from `wall` and `window` polygons.

#### 5.1.4 Phase 1 Cross-Check & Review Gate
- **Contract & Precision Audit:** Verify that `MetricFloorPlan` stores all coordinates in metres with at least $0.1\text{ mm}$ ($10^{-4}\text{ m}$) floating-point precision so the $1\text{ mm}$ ($10^{-3}\text{ m}$) checker tolerance is never triggered by rounding drift.

### Phase 2: Door & Open-Passage Topology Extraction & Parallel-Lane Portal Waypoints

#### 5.2.1 Phase 2 Goal
Construct the room adjacency graph $G_{\text{macro}} = (V_R, E_P)$ in [NetworkX](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fnetworkx.org%2Fdocumentation%2Fstable%2Freference%2Fclasses%2Fgraph.html) where rooms connect exclusively through interior `door` and `open_passage` gaps (never across walls, windows, or the `front_door` entrance), and generate parallel doorway lanes separated by $\ge 20\text{ mm}$ ($\gg 1\text{ mm}$ checker tolerance) so out-and-back runs into single-door rooms incur zero overlap ($O = 0$).

#### 5.2.2 What & How to Build in Phase 2
1. **`portal_extractor.py` (Door & Open-Passage Gap Detection):**
   - **Interior Doors:** For each `door` polygon (excluding `front_door` and any door touching the exterior of `inner`), buffer by $0.05\text{ m}$ and intersect with room polygons using a [Shapely STRtree](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fshapely.readthedocs.io%2Fen%2Fstable%2Fstrtree.html) to identify the two rooms connected by that door gap.
   - **Open Passages:** For any pair of adjacent rooms whose shared boundary inside $\mathcal{F} = \text{inner} \setminus (\text{wall} \cup \text{window})$ has an unobstructed gap of width $\ge 0.50\text{ m}$ without a `door` polygon, register an `open_passage` portal.
   - Strictly reject any adjacency across `wall`, `window`, or `front_door`.
2. **`macro_graph.py` (Topological Graph & Zero-Overlap Parallel Door Lanes):**
   - Build $G_{\text{macro}}$ over the interior rooms and verify that all eligible room node points belong to a single connected component of $\mathcal{F} = \text{inner} \setminus (\text{wall} \cup \text{window})$ (matching Slide 3 speaker note).
   - For each door or open-passage gap of width $w$ with threshold center $p_0$ and threshold unit vector $\hat{t}$:
     - Create three rectilinear portal transit tracks across the doorway gap:
       - **Center Track ($p_0$):** Used when a cable crosses the doorway only once.
       - **Parallel Lane A ($p_A = p_0 - \delta \hat{t}$) & Parallel Lane B ($p_B = p_0 + \delta \hat{t}$):** Offset by $\delta = \min(0.10\text{ m}, w / 4) \ge 0.02\text{ m}$ ($20\text{ mm}$). Because Slide 6 & 7 define Overlap ($O$) as cable lying within $1\text{ mm}$ of another cable on the same line—and explicitly state that **parallel runs on different lines do not count as overlap ($O = 0$)**—routing an out-and-back single-door room visit along Lane A (in) and Lane B (out) eliminates overlap cost $O$ completely!

#### 5.2.3 Phase 2 Verification Gate (Unit Tests & Invariant Passes)
Run `pytest tests/test_phase2_topology.py` to verify:
- **Test 2.1 (`test_slide5_door_connectivity`):** On the Slide 5 6-room schematic, verify $G_{\text{macro}}$ connects `Living`–`Bedroom 3`, `Bedroom 3`–`Bedroom 1`, `Living`–`Bedroom 1`, `Bedroom 2`–`Kitchen`, and `Kitchen`–`Storage` via doors, with `Bedroom 2` and `Storage` identified as degree-1 single-door leaf rooms.
- **Test 2.2 (`test_window_wall_entrance_rejection`):** Verify that rooms sharing only a solid wall, a window, or the `Entrance` (`front_door`) have zero direct edges in $G_{\text{macro}}$.
- **Test 2.3 (`test_parallel_door_lanes_exceed_1mm_tolerance`):** Assert that for every portal, `lane_a` and `lane_b` segments lie strictly inside $\mathcal{F} = \text{inner} \setminus (\text{wall} \cup \text{window})$ and have lateral separation $\ge 0.020\text{ m}$ ($20 \times$ the $1\text{ mm}$ overlap threshold).

#### 5.2.4 Cross-Phase Integration Check (Phase 2 $\leftrightarrow$ Phase 1) & Review Gate
- **Cross-Check with Phase 1:** Confirm every room ID in $G_{\text{macro}}$ maps to a valid metric polygon in Phase 1's `MetricFloorPlan`, and every portal threshold segment lies strictly inside `MetricFloorPlan.free_space_wkt`.
- **Phase 1 + 2 Regression Gate:** Re-run `pytest tests/test_phase1_ingestion.py tests/test_phase2_topology.py`.

### Phase 3: Obstacle-Aware Axis-Aligned Micro-Router & Directional Cost Tensor

#### 5.3.1 Phase 3 Goal
Build the axis-aligned (horizontal/vertical) pathfinding engine inside $\mathcal{F} = \text{inner} \setminus (\text{wall} \cup \text{window})$ that routes cables through door/open-passage gaps while tracking exact entry and exit directions at every node and hub so $90^\circ$ turns and $180^\circ$ reversals at nodes/hubs are accurately priced at $0.5\text{ per bend}$.

#### 5.3.2 What & How to Build in Phase 3
1. **`rectilinear_grid.py` (Exact Hanan Grid over Free Space $\mathcal{F}$):**
   - Construct an axis-aligned Hanan grid inside $\mathcal{F}$ induced by:
     - The exact $(x, y)$ coordinates of all fixed nodes $N_1, \dots, N_n$,
     - All Phase 2 portal waypoints ($p_0, p_A, p_B$ and their room-entry step points),
     - All Phase 4 candidate hub positions ($\ge 0.5\text{ m}$ from walls),
     - Parallel offset lines ($\pm 0.04\text{ m}$ around each node/portal line) so two legs can pass near a node or through a room without collinear overlap ($> 1\text{ mm}$ separation) or crossings,
     - Wall-clearance contour lines inset $0.15\text{ m}$ from interior walls.
   - Retain only horizontal ($\Delta y = 0$) and vertical ($\Delta x = 0$) grid edges whose `LineString` is strictly inside $\text{inner}$ and has zero intersection with `wall` or `window` polygons.
   - Label each grid edge with its host `room_id` (or `portal_id`) so routing for hub $H_k$ can be restricted to rooms assigned to $H_k$.
2. **`astar_rectilinear.py` (Direction-State Dijkstra / $A^*$ Router):**
   - Because Slide 6 & 7 state that **bends ($B$, $0.5$ each) are counted at vertices, nodes, and hubs (with $180^\circ$ reversals counting as $1$ bend and straight-through counting as $0$ bends)**, define the router state as $(v, d_{\text{in}})$ where $v \in V_{\text{grid}}$ and $d_{\text{in}} \in \{\text{N}, \text{S}, \text{E}, \text{W}\}$.
   - Moving from state $(u, d_1)$ to neighbor $(v, d_2)$ along axis-aligned edge $(u, v)$ incurs exact checker cost:
     $$\Delta J = \|u - v\|_1 + 0.5 \cdot \mathbb{I}(d_1 \neq d_2) + 1.0 \cdot \text{overlap\_len}(u, v) + 5.0 \cdot \text{crossings}(u, v)$$
   - Notice that under this state definition, both a $90^\circ$ turn ($d_1 \perp d_2$) and a $180^\circ$ reversal ($d_2 = -d_1$) satisfy $d_1 \neq d_2$ and therefore incur exactly $+0.5$ ($1$ bend), matching Slide 7's rule (`All turns are 90°, and a reversal counts once`)!
3. **`cost_matrix.py` (Directional Pairwise Cost Tensor):**
   - For every pair of points $(u, v)$ (nodes and hub candidates) and every pair of entry/exit directions $(d_{\text{start}}, d_{\text{end}}) \in \{\text{N}, \text{S}, \text{E}, \text{W}\}^2$, precompute and cache the minimum rectilinear cost $C(u, d_{\text{start}} \to v, d_{\text{end}})$ and compressed polyline.

### Phase 4: Lexicographic Hub Placement & Contiguous Zone Partitioning

#### 5.4.1 Phase 4 Goal
Solve for the minimum feasible number of hubs $H \ge H_{\min} = \max(\lceil \text{area}/100 \rceil, \lceil \text{nodes}/5 \rceil)$, partition all eligible rooms into $H$ door-connected zones satisfying $\text{Area}(Z_k) \le 100.0\text{ m}^2$ and $|N(Z_k)| \le 5$, place each hub $H_k$ inside one of its assigned eligible rooms at $\ge 0.5\text{ m}$ from walls, and incorporate the Slide 3/6 tie-breaker (minimizing the assigned-area gap between hubs).

#### 5.4.2 What & How to Build in Phase 4
1. **`hub_candidates.py` (Rule 6 Compliant Hub Candidate Generator):**
   - For each eligible room $R_i$, compute the $0.5\text{ m}$ wall-cleared interior polygon:
     $$P_i^{\text{hub}} = R_i \setminus (\text{wall} \cup \text{window}).\text{buffer}(0.505\text{ m})$$
     (using $0.505\text{ m}$ to provide a $5\text{ mm}$ safety margin above the $0.5\text{ m} \pm 1\text{ mm}$ checker threshold).
   - Sample candidate hub points $\mathcal{H}_i \subset P_i^{\text{hub}}$ aligned with the Hanan coordinates of $N_i$ and the room's doorways (so a leg from $H_k$ to $N_i$ or to a doorway is a straight line with $0$ extra bends), while keeping $\|H_k - N_i\|_2 \ge 0.30\text{ m}$ so hub and node markers remain visually distinct in the same room.
2. **`zone_milp.py` (Exact Contiguous Partitioner with Lexicographic Objective):**
   - Because ResPlan plans have **at most $10$ eligible rooms** (Slide 2 speaker notes), the exact contiguous partitioning problem over $G_{\text{macro}}$ can be solved to **100% global optimality in $< 50\text{ ms}$** using either [Google OR-Tools CP-SAT](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fdevelopers.google.com%2Foptimization%2Fcp%2Fcp_solver) (with Single-Commodity Flow contiguity constraints) or exact connected-subgraph enumeration + Set Partitioning MILP:
     - **Step 1 (Minimum Hub Count $H$):** Start at $H = H_{\min} = \max(\lceil \sum A_i / 100 \rceil, \lceil N / 5 \rceil)$.
     - **Step 2 (Valid Connected Zone Generation):** Enumerate or constrain all connected room subsets $Z \subseteq V_R$ on $G_{\text{macro}}$ whose eligible rooms $Z_{\text{elig}} = Z \cap R_{\text{elig}}$ satisfy:
       1. $1 \le |Z_{\text{elig}}| \le 5$ (Rule 2 node capacity),
       2. $\sum_{r \in Z_{\text{elig}}} A_r \le 100.0\text{ m}^2$ (Rule 2 area capacity),
       3. $G_{\text{macro}}[Z]$ is connected via doors/open passages only (Rule 3 zone contiguity). (Note: per Slide 2 & 3, every hub's assigned rooms are eligible rooms connected through doors/open passages; if an interior corridor/hallway is represented as an open passage or transit space, verify contiguity across doors/passages).
     - **Step 3 (Exact Zone Cost Evaluation):** For each candidate valid zone $Z$ (which has $\le 5$ nodes!), invoke Phase 5's fast exact Held-Karp ring solver over candidate hub points $h \in \bigcup_{r \in Z_{\text{elig}}} \mathcal{H}_r$ to obtain the **true exact minimum ring cost $J^*(Z)$ and optimal hub point $H^*(Z)$**!
     - **Step 4 (Exact Set Partitioning with Area-Gap Tie-Break):** Select $H$ disjoint valid zones $Z_1, \dots, Z_H$ covering all $R_{\text{elig}}$ ($\bigsqcup_{k=1}^H Z_{k,\text{elig}} = R_{\text{elig}}$) that minimize the combined lexicographic objective:
       $$\min \sum_{k=1}^{H} J^*(Z_k) + 10^{-3} \cdot \left( \max_{k} A(Z_k) - \min_{k} A(Z_k) \right)$$
     - If no valid partition of size $H$ exists, increment $H \leftarrow H + 1$ and repeat.
   - **Why This Exact Decomposition Is Superior for ResPlan:** Because $|R_{\text{elig}}| \le 10$ and $|Z_{\text{elig}}| \le 5$, pricing each connected zone with its **exact closed-ring cost $J^*(Z)$** eliminates any gap between the zone partitioner and the physical router—achieving true **joint optimization** of hub count, zone assignment, hub location, node sequence, and cable route!

### Phase 5: Direction-Aware Closed-Ring Sequencing & Zero-Overlap Route Synthesis

#### 5.5.1 Phase 5 Goal
For each hub $H_k$ and its assigned nodes $\{N_{k,1}, \dots, N_{k,m}\}$ ($m \le 5$), determine the exact visiting order $H_k \to N_{k,\sigma(1)} \to \dots \to N_{k,\sigma(m)} \to H_k$ and synthesize the physical rectilinear polylines that minimize $J_k = L_k + 5X_k + O_k + 0.5B_k$, explicitly accounting for bends at nodes/hubs and using parallel lines ($> 1\text{ mm}$ apart) on retraced single-door rooms so $O_k = 0$ and $X_k = 0$.

#### 5.5.2 What & How to Build in Phase 5
1. **`exact_ring_tsp.py` (Direction-Aware Exact Ring Solver for $m \le 5$):**
   - Because each hub has $m \le 5$ assigned nodes, there are at most $5! = 120$ node permutations and $4^{m+1}$ arrival/departure directions at the hub and nodes.
   - Implement a direction-aware dynamic program over $(S, u, d_{\text{in}}, d_{\text{hub\_start}})$ where:
     - $S \subseteq \{1, \dots, m\}$ is the bitmask of visited nodes,
     - $u \in \{1, \dots, m\}$ is the current node,
     - $d_{\text{in}} \in \{\text{N}, \text{S}, \text{E}, \text{W}\}$ is the direction in which the cable arrived at $u$,
     - $d_{\text{hub\_start}} \in \{\text{N}, \text{S}, \text{E}, \text{W}\}$ is the initial departure direction from hub $H_k$.
   - When transitioning from $(u, d_{\text{in}})$ to next node $v$ by departing $u$ in direction $d_{\text{out}}$:
     - Add $+0.5$ bend cost at node $u$ if $d_{\text{out}} \neq d_{\text{in}}$ (counting both $90^\circ$ turns and $180^\circ$ reversals at node $u$ per Slide 7!).
     - At the final return leg from $u_{\text{last}}$ back to $H_k$ arriving in direction $d_{\text{hub\_end}}$, add $+0.5$ bend cost at hub $H_k$ if $d_{\text{hub\_end}} \neq d_{\text{hub\_start}}$!
2. **`loop_synthesizer.py` (Sequential Conflict-Free Polyline Synthesis):**
   - Synthesize the legs of the optimal sequence $v_0 (= H_k) \to v_1 \to \dots \to v_m \to v_{m+1} (= H_k)$ sequentially on the zone's rectilinear grid `G_rect_zone`:
     - **Parallel Runs in Single-Door Rooms (Slide 5 & 7 Optimization):** When a leg enters and exits a single-door room (like `Bedroom 2` or `Storage` on Slide 5), route the incoming leg along Door **Lane A** and the outgoing leg along Door **Lane B** (separated by $\ge 20\text{ mm} > 1\text{ mm}$). Per Slide 7, parallel runs separated by $> 1\text{ mm}$ have **Overlap $O = 0$**, whereas retracing on the exact same line would incur $O = 1.0/\text{m}$!
     - **Dynamic Usage Penalties:** After routing each leg $s$, mark all grid segments within $1\text{ mm}$ of leg $s$ with an overlap surcharge ($+1.0/\text{m}$) and all interior vertices of leg $s$ (excluding node/hub endpoints) with a crossing surcharge ($+5.0\text{ per crossing}$) before routing leg $s+1$.
     - **Collinear Vertex Simplification:** Merge consecutive collinear segments in each leg's `polyline` so every interior vertex in `polyline` represents a true $90^\circ$ turn.

#### 5.6.2 What & How to Build in Phase 6
